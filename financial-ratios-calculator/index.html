Book value per share

Book Value Per Share = (Total Equity − Preferred Equity) ÷ Shares Outstanding

Book value per share is the accounting value backing each common share. You calculate book value per share by taking total shareholder equity, removing any preferred equity that ranks ahead of common holders, and dividing by the common shares outstanding. If equity is 10 million dollars with no preferred stock and there are 1 million shares, the figure is 10 dollars. Investors compare it to the market price: a share trading well below its book value may be cheap, or may signal that the market doubts the stated asset values. Tangible net worth

Tangible Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities − Intangible Assets

The tangible net worth formula strips intangibles such as goodwill, patents, and brand value out of equity to show the hard asset base of a business. Lenders rely on it because intangibles rarely survive a forced sale, so this number better reflects what could be recovered. A firm with 500,000 dollars in assets, 200,000 in liabilities, and 50,000 of intangibles has a tangible net worth of 250,000 dollars.

ROCE, return on capital employed

ROCE = EBIT ÷ (Total Assets − Current Liabilities) × 100

The ROCE formula measures how efficiently a company turns its long term capital into operating profit. Divide earnings before interest and tax by capital employed, which is total assets minus current liabilities, then express it as a percentage. A return on capital employed of 25 percent means the business earns 25 cents of operating profit per dollar of capital it ties up. It is most useful compared across firms in the same industry or against a company's own history.

WACC, weighted average cost of capital

WACC = (E ÷ V × Cost of Equity) + (D ÷ V × Cost of Debt × (1 − Tax Rate))

WACC is the blended rate a company pays to finance itself across both equity and debt, weighted by how much of each it uses. Because interest is tax deductible, the debt portion is multiplied by one minus the tax rate. A business funded 60 percent by equity costing 10 percent and 40 percent by debt costing 5 percent, taxed at 21 percent, has a WACC near 7.58 percent. Analysts use it as the discount rate when valuing future cash flows, so a lower WACC raises what a company is worth.

Equity multiplier

Equity Multiplier = Total Assets ÷ Total Shareholder Equity

The equity multiplier formula shows how heavily a company leans on debt to fund its assets. Total assets divided by equity gives a figure where 2.5 means 2.50 dollars of assets sit on every dollar of equity, with liabilities covering the gap. A higher multiplier means more leverage, which lifts returns in good years and deepens losses in bad ones. It is one of the three components of the DuPont breakdown of return on equity.

Capital gains yield

Capital Gains Yield = (Ending Price − Beginning Price) ÷ Beginning Price × 100

The capital gains yield formula isolates the price appreciation of an investment, ignoring income. Buy a share at 50 dollars, watch it reach 60, and the capital gains yield is 20 percent. It pairs naturally with dividend yield: together they make up an investment's total return. Use it when you want to separate how much of your gain came from price movement rather than payouts.

Holding period return

Holding Period Return = (Income + (Ending − Beginning)) ÷ Beginning × 100

The holding period yield formula captures the complete return over the time you owned an asset, both price change and income. A stock that climbed from 100 to 110 dollars and paid a 5 dollar dividend delivered a 15 percent holding period return, versus a 10 percent capital gains yield alone. The extra 5 points is the dividend. This is the honest measure of what an investment actually returned in hand.

Real rate of return

Real Rate of Return = ((1 + Nominal Rate) ÷ (1 + Inflation Rate)) − 1

To calculate the real rate of return, adjust a nominal return for inflation rather than simply subtracting it. A 7 percent nominal return against 3 percent inflation gives about 3.88 percent, slightly below the 4 percent that subtraction would suggest, because the precise formula accounts for compounding. This is the number that reflects real growth in purchasing power, and our inflation calculator shows the same erosion applied to a dollar amount over time.

Dividend per share

Dividend Per Share = Total Dividends Paid ÷ Shares Outstanding

Dividend per share tells you the cash a company distributes on each share over a period. Divide total dividends paid to common holders by the shares outstanding: 500,000 dollars across 1 million shares is 50 cents per share. Track it over several years to see whether a payout is growing, flat, or being cut, which often says more about management confidence than a single year's figure does.

Contribution margin per unit

Contribution Margin = Sale Price Per Unit − Variable Cost Per Unit

Contribution margin per unit is the amount each sale contributes toward fixed costs and profit after covering its own variable cost. Sell an item for 50 dollars that costs 30 in materials and labor, and each unit contributes 20 dollars. It is the foundation of break even analysis, since dividing fixed costs by the contribution margin tells you how many units you must sell before you start making a profit. SavingsClub Tips
1
A ratio means little in isolation. Compare it to the same company over several years, or to rivals in the same industry. A 25 percent ROCE is strong for a utility and weak for a software firm.
2
Match the source documents. Balance sheet figures like assets and equity are point in time snapshots, while income statement figures like EBIT cover a period. Mixing dates quietly distorts a ratio.
3
Watch for intangibles. Two firms with identical book value can differ hugely once goodwill is removed. Run tangible net worth alongside book value before drawing conclusions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate book value per share?

Book value per share equals total shareholder equity minus preferred equity, divided by the number of common shares outstanding. If a company has 10 million dollars of common equity and 1 million shares, the book value per share is 10 dollars. It represents the accounting value backing each share, and investors compare it to the market price to judge whether a stock trades above or below its net asset value.

What is the ROCE formula?

Return on capital employed equals earnings before interest and tax, or EBIT, divided by capital employed, where capital employed is total assets minus current liabilities. The result is shown as a percentage. A firm with 200,000 dollars of EBIT and 800,000 dollars of capital employed has an ROCE of 25 percent, meaning it generates 25 cents of operating profit for every dollar of long term capital it uses.

How is WACC calculated?

The weighted average cost of capital blends the cost of equity and the after tax cost of debt by their share of total financing. The formula is the equity weight times the cost of equity, plus the debt weight times the cost of debt times one minus the tax rate. A company financed 60 percent by equity at 10 percent and 40 percent by debt at 5 percent, taxed at 21 percent, has a WACC of about 7.58 percent.

What is the formula for tangible net worth?

Tangible net worth equals total assets minus total liabilities minus intangible assets such as goodwill, patents, and trademarks. It strips out items that cannot easily be sold to show the hard asset value backing a business. Lenders lean on this figure because it reflects what could realistically be recovered, rather than accounting values that may not survive a sale.

How do you calculate capital gains yield?

Capital gains yield is the ending price minus the beginning price, divided by the beginning price, shown as a percentage. A share bought at 50 dollars and now worth 60 dollars has a capital gains yield of 20 percent. It measures only the price appreciation of an investment, separate from any dividend income, which the holding period return captures instead.

What is the difference between capital gains yield and holding period return?

Capital gains yield counts only the change in price. Holding period return adds any income received, such as dividends or interest, to that price change before dividing by the starting value. A stock that rose from 100 to 110 dollars and paid a 5 dollar dividend has a 10 percent capital gains yield but a 15 percent holding period return. The gap is the income component.

How do you calculate the real rate of return?

The real rate of return adjusts a nominal return for inflation using the formula one plus the nominal rate divided by one plus the inflation rate, minus one. A 7 percent nominal return with 3 percent inflation gives a real return of about 3.88 percent, not 4 percent, because the simple subtraction ignores compounding. The real figure shows the true growth in purchasing power.

What is the equity multiplier formula?

The equity multiplier equals total assets divided by total shareholder equity. It shows how much of a company's assets are financed by equity versus debt. A multiplier of 2.5 means the firm holds 2.50 dollars of assets for every dollar of equity, with the rest funded by liabilities. A higher figure signals more leverage and, with it, more financial risk.

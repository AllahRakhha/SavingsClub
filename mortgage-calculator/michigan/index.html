The Michigan real estate transfer tax

When a deed records in this state, the seller normally pays a levy on the sale price. It has two layers. The state portion runs 3.75 dollars for every 500 dollars of value, set by Public Act 330 of 1993. On top of that, counties add 0.55 dollars per 500, the figure that applies across Kent County and the Grand Rapids metro. Combined, that is 4.30 dollars per 500, close to 0.86 percent of what a home sells for.

A quirk worth knowing: the charge is figured in 500 dollar blocks, and any leftover fraction rounds up to a full block. A 250,001 dollar deal counts as 501 increments, not 500, nudging the bill slightly higher. Our tool applies that rounding exactly as the register of deeds does, so the number you see matches the paperwork.

Grand Rapids versus Detroit: why county matters

One place the amount diverges is population. Wayne County, which contains Detroit, is permitted a higher local rate of 0.75 dollars per 500 rather than the usual 0.55. Everywhere else, including the entire west side around Grand Rapids, keeps the standard figure. That gap is why the calculator asks you to pick a county before it computes anything. Choosing Detroit for a west Michigan purchase would overstate your obligation, and the reverse would leave you short at settlement.

Exemptions and the first time buyer refund

Not every deed triggers the charge. Transfers between spouses, gifts to a child or parent for little consideration, and any sale under 100 dollars are exempt outright. Separately, someone purchasing a principal residence who has not owned real property in the prior three years may claim a state refund after closing rather than skipping the tax up front. These provisions have specific paperwork, so raise them early with whoever handles your settlement.

Planning your monthly number in Kent County

Property assessments here deserve a note of their own. Michigan limits how fast the taxable value of a primary residence can rise each year through Proposal A and the Headlee amendment, which means a longtime owner and a fresh purchaser on the same street can face very different bills. When you buy, the assessment often resets, so budget from current millage rather than the seller's old figure. Effective rates around the Grand Rapids area generally range from 1.3 to 1.8 percent depending on city and school district, and entering your specific local rate will give the truest monthly estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the transfer tax on a Michigan home?

Michigan charges a combined transfer tax of 4.30 dollars per 500 of sale price, which works out to about 0.86 percent. That splits into a state portion of 3.75 dollars per 500 and a county portion of 0.55 dollars per 500 in most areas, including Grand Rapids. On a 325,000 dollar sale the total is roughly 2,795 dollars. The seller pays it by default, though the purchase agreement can shift it.

Does Michigan have a mortgage tax like New York?

No. Unlike some states, Michigan imposes no separate mortgage recording tax on the loan itself. The only state level charge tied to a sale is the real estate transfer tax, based on the price rather than the borrowed amount, and it is customarily a seller expense. That keeps buyer closing costs here lower than in high tax markets.

What is a typical monthly mortgage payment in Grand Rapids?

It depends on price, down payment, and rate, but a Grand Rapids home near the local median with 20 percent down and a 30 year loan commonly lands somewhere in the 1,900 to 2,300 dollar range once property tax and insurance are added. Enter your own figures above for a precise estimate, since Kent County millage rates vary by city and school district.

Who pays the transfer tax when selling a house in Michigan?

By long standing custom and under state law the seller is responsible, and title companies collect it at closing before the deed records. In a slower market a seller may agree to cover part of the buyer costs instead, or the two sides may split the charge. The obligation is negotiable and belongs in the signed purchase agreement.

Are there transfer tax exemptions for Michigan buyers?

Several transfers are fully exempt, including those between spouses, gifts to a child or parent with little consideration, and sales under 100 dollars. First time principal residence buyers who have not owned property in the prior three years may qualify for a state transfer tax refund rather than a full exemption. Confirm eligibility with your closing agent.

What property tax rate should I expect in Kent County?

Kent County effective property tax rates commonly fall between 1.3 and 1.8 percent of value, varying by municipality and school district within the Grand Rapids area. Michigan also caps annual taxable value growth on your primary residence through the Headlee and Proposal A limits, so your bill can differ from a new buyer's. Use your local millage for the closest figure.

How do I calculate my Michigan mortgage payment?

Start with the loan amount, which is the price minus your down payment. Apply your interest rate and term to get the principal and interest portion, then add monthly property tax and homeowners insurance, plus private mortgage insurance if you put down under 20 percent. The tool above does all of this and shows each piece separately.

Is the Michigan transfer tax based on the loan or the price?

The price. The charge is assessed on the full consideration paid for the property, divided into 500 dollar increments with any fraction rounded up. This differs from a mortgage based levy, so a larger down payment does not reduce it. Only a lower purchase price, or a qualifying exemption, changes the amount owed.

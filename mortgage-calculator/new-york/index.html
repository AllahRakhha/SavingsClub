NYC mortgage recording tax, explained

The NYC mortgage recording tax is a tax on your loan amount, not your purchase price, paid by the buyer when the mortgage is recorded. For one to three family homes and condos in the five boroughs, the buyer facing rate is 1.8 percent on loans under 500,000 dollars and 1.925 percent on loans of 500,000 dollars or more. On a 600,000 dollar loan that is 11,550 dollars, usually the single largest line on a New York City buyer's closing statement.

Two facts change the number in ways a plain mortgage tax calculator NYC search will not tell you. First, co-ops are exempt, because you are buying shares in a corporation rather than real property, so no mortgage is recorded. Second, the tax is only on financed purchases, so a cash buyer pays none of it. If you are comparing a condo against a co-op, this tax alone can be tens of thousands of dollars of difference.

Outside New York City the rate is far lower

The high 1.8 to 1.925 percent figures apply only inside the five boroughs. If you are buying in Westchester, on Long Island, or upstate, you pay only the state portion plus a county surcharge, which lands around 1.05 to 1.3 percent depending on the county. That is why this calculator asks for your location first. Choosing the wrong one can misstate your closing cost by thousands of dollars, so the NYS mortgage tax calculator path and the NYC path are kept separate here.

The NYC mansion tax and its cliff

The mansion tax is a separate buyer tax on residential purchases of 1,000,000 dollars or more in New York City. It is progressive, running from 1 percent up to 3.9 percent as the price climbs, and it applies to the entire purchase price rather than just the amount over a million.

The threshold is a genuine cliff, not a gentle ramp. A purchase at 999,999 dollars owes no mansion tax. A purchase at exactly 1,000,000 dollars owes 1 percent of the full million, which is 10,000 dollars. That single dollar of price triggers ten thousand dollars of tax, which is why buyers near the line negotiate hard to stay just under it. SavingsClub Tips
1
Budget the recording tax as cash, not as part of the loan. It is due at closing and cannot be financed into the mortgage. On a New York City purchase it is often the largest single closing cost, so it needs to be sitting in your account before you sign.
2
Near 1 million dollars, negotiate the price down, not up. The mansion tax cliff means a price of 1,000,000 dollars costs 10,000 dollars more in tax than 999,999 dollars. If you are close, that threshold is worth real effort at the negotiating table.
3
Compare a co-op against a condo on total cost. Co-ops skip the recording tax entirely, which can offset a higher price. Run both through the calculator before deciding which is genuinely cheaper for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the New York mortgage recording tax?

The mortgage recording tax is a tax on the loan amount, paid by the buyer at closing when a new mortgage is recorded. In New York City the buyer rate is 1.8 percent on loans under 500,000 dollars and 1.925 percent on loans of 500,000 dollars or more, for one to three family homes and condos. Outside the city the rate is far lower, roughly 1.05 percent depending on the county. It is often the single largest closing cost for a New York City buyer.

How do I calculate NYC mortgage recording tax?

Multiply your loan amount by the applicable rate. For a New York City loan under 500,000 dollars, multiply by 1.8 percent. For a loan of 500,000 dollars or more, multiply by 1.925 percent. For example, a 600,000 dollar loan is taxed at 1.925 percent, which is 11,550 dollars. The calculator on this page does this automatically once you enter your loan amount and choose your location.

What is the NYC mansion tax in 2026?

The mansion tax is a buyer tax on residential purchases of 1,000,000 dollars or more in New York City. It is progressive, starting at 1 percent and rising to 3.9 percent as the price increases. It applies to the entire purchase price, not just the amount above 1 million. A purchase at exactly 1,000,000 dollars owes 10,000 dollars, while a purchase at 999,999 dollars owes nothing, so the threshold is a sharp cliff.

Do co-ops pay the NYC mortgage recording tax?

No. Co-op purchases are exempt from the mortgage recording tax because you are buying shares in a corporation rather than real property, so no mortgage is recorded against a deed. This is one of the financial differences between co-ops and condos in New York City. The mansion tax, however, still applies to a co-op purchase of 1 million dollars or more.

How much are total closing costs for a New York City buyer?

For a financed condo or house purchase in New York City, buyer closing costs commonly run about 4 percent of the price, driven largely by the mortgage recording tax and, above 1 million dollars, the mansion tax. Co-op closing costs are lower, around 2 percent, because there is no recording tax. Title insurance, lender fees, and attorney fees make up the rest.

Who pays the mortgage recording tax in New York?

The buyer pays the mortgage recording tax at closing. The lender contributes a separate 0.25 percent special additional portion on qualifying residential loans, which is why the buyer facing rates are 1.8 and 1.925 percent rather than the full combined figure. Cash buyers pay no recording tax at all because there is no mortgage to record.

What is the average property tax rate in New York?

Property tax varies widely by location. New York City has a relatively low effective rate near 0.9 percent, while Long Island and Westchester County are among the highest in the nation at 2 percent or more. The statewide average effective rate is roughly 1.7 percent. Because the range is so wide, enter your own local figure in the calculator rather than relying on a statewide average.

Can I avoid the New York mortgage recording tax?

There are limited legal ways to reduce it. A Consolidation, Extension and Modification Agreement, known as a CEMA, can lower the tax on a refinance or a purchase CEMA by treating part of the existing mortgage as assigned rather than newly recorded. Buying a co-op avoids the tax entirely, and an all cash purchase avoids it because no mortgage is recorded. These options do not fit every situation, so confirm with your attorney.
